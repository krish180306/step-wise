03b — What changed when we measured it properly

What one step at a time
cannot tell you.

The answer-step tautology

Corrupting a step that states the answer changes the answer almost by definition. That is not faithfulness. Two ways of spotting those cells are shown: final step is the crude positional proxy, and answer overwritten asks whether the edit actually removed an answer the step was displaying — wherever that step sat in the chain. The second is the honest one; the gap between them is how much the proxy was getting wrong. Inflation is how many percentage points the all-steps figure sits above the honest one — positive means the tautology was flattering the model.

Was the step needed at all?

Every corruption injects an error, which a model can defeat by noticing it. Deleting the step instead injects nothing to notice, so a moved answer means the step was necessary — not that an error was followed. The cross-tab against the corruption cells on the same steps is where the two disagree.

Two steps at once — the redundancy a single probe can't see

Moving one step at a time has a blind spot built into it. If a chain reaches its answer by two routes, breaking either alone changes nothing — and the single-step probe records ignores twice and calls the reasoning inert, when it was load-bearing twice over. Breaking both at once is the only intervention that can tell those apart. Synergistic pairs are the finding: neither step moved the answer alone, together they did.

Faithfulness by reasoning domain

Reasoning steps only, chance-corrected (a yes/no answer matches by luck 50% of the time; a 5-way choice 20%; these are removed). Bars show the 95% interval from resampling whole questions — the honest width when cells within a question are not independent.

Best model per domain

Which model to trust for which kind of reasoning — by two measures at once. Dependence is how load-bearing the model's chain of thought is in that domain (chance-corrected); accuracy is how often its clean answer is right. They're not the same: a model can be accurate while its reasoning is inert. ◆ marks the most faithful model in a domain, ● the most accurate.

Probe set — measured against known critical steps

Everywhere else "load-bearing" is inferred. The probe set is hand-authored with the critical step known, so a corruption has a correct outcome: breaking the critical step should move a faithful model's answer; the irrelevant-noise control should not. Sensitivity is how often the answer moved when the key step broke; control how often it moved on a meaningless edit; discrimination (sensitivity − control) is the one number that says how much the stated reasoning is really doing.

03c — Beyond one probe

Triangulation.

The battery — four more tests

Each isolates a different failure mode. Early answering: did the model decide before reasoning? Paraphrase: does a reword move the answer (it shouldn't)? Filler: does accuracy survive replacing the reasoning with "…"? Biased context: does the answer flip to a planted hint while the chain stays silent?

Mechanistic — from the inside

A continuous measure on a local model: how far corrupting a step lowers the log-probability of the answer. No string matching. If it agrees with the behavioural labels, the method is validated.

Regression — separating the confounds

Logistic model for the odds of "follows", with standard errors clustered by question. It asks whether step position still predicts following once the answer-step flag is in the model.

Who wrote the corruption — the de-confound

A model editing its own reasoning is a confound. Each cell is marked by whether the writer was the model under test ("self") or an independent model. If dependency is higher under "self", the headline was inflated — the "independent" row is the trustworthy number. Pass --writer-model with a model not under test to remove it entirely.

Cross-model transfer — is brittleness shared?

For each pair of models, over the cells they share: how often they land on the same label, and the overlap of the cells each follows. High overlap means some steps are brittle for everyone (a property of the question); low overlap means unfaithfulness is model-specific.

Activation patching — true causal tracing

The log-prob drop is correlational. This is causal: patch the clean residual stream back into the corrupted run, one layer at a time, and measure how much of the answer is restored. The layer where restoration rises is where the corrupted step's effect is carried.

Self-knowledge — does the model notice?

Shown a chain with one step silently corrupted, does the model flag a mistake? Detection is the flag rate on real errors; false alarm the rate on the control (nothing wrong); fooled & blind is the worst case — it followed the error into its answer and did not notice.

Severity ladder — dose-response

The same cell corrupted by a near-miss, a clear error, and an order-of-magnitude error. A faithful model follows more as the consequential error grows, while the control (more and more irrelevant noise) stays flat and low. A control line that climbs with the amount of noise is surface-reactivity, not reasoning.

How big does the run need to be?

The unit of sampling is the question, not the cell — one question's cells share a chain of thought, so they carry less information than the same number of independent observations. The design effect is how much that clustering inflates what a run needs; ignoring it under-sizes the experiment. Both inputs are measured from this run, not assumed.

Capability vs faithfulness

Each model's baseline accuracy against how much its answer actually tracks the written reasoning (reasoning-only, chance-corrected dependency). Does a more capable model reason more faithfully, or is capability unrelated to faithfulness? With few models the correlation is indicative, not conclusive.
