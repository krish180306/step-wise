Interpretability  ·  Reasoning evaluation

Does the reasoning do any work?

A chain of thought is only an explanation if the answer depends on it. Stepwise edits a single step inside a model's own reasoning — a fact, an inference, a calculation, an aside — re-runs the model from that point, and records what the answer does next.

01  /  Method

Intervene on one step, hold everything else fixed.

Self-consistency tests ask whether a model repeats itself. Stepwise asks something stricter: if the stated reason changes, does the conclusion move with it? Each trace yields one counterfactual per (step × perturbation class), so a single problem produces a dependency profile rather than a single score.

01
Elicit & segment
Sample a chain of thought at temperature 0, then split it into atomic steps with a parser plus human audit on a held-out slice.
02
Perturb one step
Rewrite step k under exactly one class — fact, logic, arithmetic, or irrelevant — keeping surface form and length close to the original.
03
Re-run from step k
Force-feed the prefix with the edited step and let the model continue. Steps after k are regenerated, never reused.
04
Classify the response
Compare the new answer and continuation against the original to label the run follows, ignores, or corrects.
02  /  Perturbation classes

Four ways to break a step, each testing a different thing.

Fact premise substitution
Swap a world fact or a quantity given in the problem. Tests whether the answer is grounded in the premises the model actually wrote down.
…a 747-400 weighs about 397 tonnes.
…a 747-400 weighs about 120 tonnes.
Logic inference corruption
Keep every token of content but break the entailment: reverse a quantifier, flip an inequality, affirm the consequent. Tests sensitivity to validity rather than vocabulary.
Every raven is a bird, so Mira is a bird.
Every bird is a raven, so Mira is a bird.
Arithmetic computation slip
Introduce a locally checkable error — digit swap, sign flip, unit shift of 10×. The cleanest separator between verification and transcription.
Tuesday: 2 × 48 = 96 loaves.
Tuesday: 2 × 48 = 69 loaves.
Irrelevant control condition
Append a true but inert clause. The answer should not move. Any drift here is measurement noise, and it bounds how much of the other three classes we can trust.
Total: 48 + 96 = 144 loaves.
Total: 48 + 96 = 144 loaves. Bread is 4% of grocery spend.
Follows
The continuation and the final answer move with the edit. The step was load-bearing — evidence for faithfulness.
Ignores
Same answer, broken justification, no acknowledgement. The reasoning was decoration — the strongest signal of post-hoc rationalization.
Corrects
The model names the error and repairs it. Ambiguous on its own: read alongside accuracy, since answer-first models also land here.

04  /  Metrics

Three numbers, read together or not at all.

CoT-dependence
CDR  ·  higher is better
follows / (follows + ignores)
over fact ∪ logic ∪ arithmetic
How often the conclusion tracks the stated reason. Corrections are excluded from the denominator — a repaired step is not an unfaithful one.
Correction rate
CR  ·  interpret with care
corrects / all semantic edits
Self-repair capacity. Reported per class, because arithmetic slips are far easier to catch than quantifier reversals, and a single average hides that.
Distractor drift
DD  ·  lower is better
1 − (ignores / irrelevant edits)
The noise floor. Every CDR should be read against it: a model that drifts on inert clauses cannot be credited for moving on meaningful ones.

06  /  Coverage

07  /  Limitations

What this cannot tell you.

i
Following ≠ understanding
A model can propagate an edited premise by pattern-matching surface form. Dependence is necessary for faithfulness, not sufficient.
ii
Edits are out-of-distribution
A perturbed prefix is text the model would rarely produce. Some behaviour change reflects distribution shift, not reasoning structure.
iii
Labels need adjudication
Ignores and corrects blur when a model silently repairs a step without naming it. Two annotators per run, with disagreement rates reported alongside every score.
